Carnot efficiency

A reversible engine between reservoirs at absolute temperatures \(T_h\) and \(T_c\) has efficiency \(\eta = 1 - T_c/T_h\).

Seven kinds of card can test it. Fact and Equation are on by default. Turn on the others in step 2.

Factdefault
What kind of temperature goes into the Carnot efficiency?
Absolute temperature, in kelvin.
Equationdefault
What is the efficiency of a reversible engine between \(T_h\) and \(T_c\)?
\(\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_c}{T_h}\)
Stepsoptional
How do you find the best possible efficiency from two reservoir temperatures?
Convert both to kelvin, divide cold by hot, subtract from 1.
Applyoptional
A plant raises steam at 550 K and cools it with a river at 290 K. What caps its efficiency?
The Carnot limit, \(1 - 290/550 \approx 47\%\).
Whyoptional
Why can no engine between two reservoirs beat a reversible one?
It could drive the reversible engine backwards and break the Kelvin-Planck statement.
Composeoptional
All reversible engines between two reservoirs share one efficiency, and for them \(Q_c/Q_h = T_c/T_h\). What is that efficiency?
\(\eta = 1 - Q_c/Q_h = 1 - T_c/T_h\)
Contrastoptional
Which can exceed 1: an engine's efficiency or a refrigerator's coefficient of performance?
The coefficient of performance. Efficiency is always below 1.
