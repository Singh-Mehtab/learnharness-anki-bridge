One idea from your graph

Carnot efficiency

A reversible engine between reservoirs at absolute temperatures \(T_h\) and \(T_c\) has efficiency \(\eta = 1 - T_c/T_h\).

Six kinds of card can test it. Fact and Equation are on by default. Why and Steps are switched on in the Write step, Apply and Contrast in Settings.

Factdefault
What kind of temperature goes into the Carnot efficiency?
Absolute temperature, in kelvin.
Equationdefault
What is the efficiency of a reversible engine between \(T_h\) and \(T_c\)?
\(\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_c}{T_h}\)
WhyWrite step
Why can no engine between two reservoirs beat a reversible one?
It could drive the reversible engine backwards and break the Kelvin-Planck statement.
StepsWrite step
Finding an engine's Carnot limit, you have converted both reservoir temperatures to kelvin. What next?
Divide \(T_c\) by \(T_h\).
ApplySettings
A plant raises steam at 550 K and cools it with a river at 290 K. Which relation caps its efficiency, and what tells you?
The Carnot efficiency. Heat flows between two reservoirs at fixed temperatures.
ContrastSettings
A reversible device between 300 K and 600 K scores 0.5 on its figure of merit. What kind of device is it?
A heat engine, \(\eta = 1 - 300/600\). A refrigerator there would have a COP of 1.
